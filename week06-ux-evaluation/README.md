# Week 6 — UX Evaluation Brief
## Hill Country Trail Guide

**Primary User:** Maya Torres  
**Primary Task:** Choose a beginner-appropriate Saturday hike that can be completed in about three hours or less.

---

## 1. Task Walkthrough
Briefly describe what Maya would try to do first, what she would look for, and where she might hesitate.

Maya would probably start by looking at the trail pictures to see which ones look interesting and beginner-friendly. She would then look at the difficulty, distance, elevation, and how long the hike might take. She might hesitate because there aren't actual trail photos to look at, and because the difficulty isn't explained clearly or when she can't tell if a trail can be finished in about three hours. She could also have a harder time comparing the trails on her phone.

---

## 2. Five UX Findings

Document **exactly five meaningful findings**.

### Finding 1: No estimated hiking time

**Observation:**
The trail cards show the distance and elevation, but they don't show how long the hike is expected to take.

**Evidence:**
The trails show information like "4.8 mi · 620 ft" and "3.2 mi · 840 ft," but there is no estimated hiking time.

**User Impact:**
Maya is a beginner, so she might not know how long a certain distance or elevation will take her. She would have to guess if the hike can be finished in about three hours.

**Principle:**
Clarity and cognitive load.

**Priority:** High

**Recommendation:**
Make the estimated hiking time easier to find when comparing trails.

### Finding 2: Difficulty labels don't give much information
**Observation:**
The trails are labeled "Easy" or "Moderate," but the labels don't really explain what that means.

**Evidence:**
For example, a trail can be labeled "Easy" but still have rocky areas or other conditions mentioned in the longer description.

**User Impact:**
Maya doesn't have much hiking experience, so she may not know what to expect from an "Easy" or "Moderate" trail. She could choose something that is harder than she expected.

**Principle:**
Clarity and plain language.

**Priority:** High

**Recommendation:**
Give more useful and beginner-friendly information about what makes a trail easy or moderate.
  

### Finding 3: The trail images don't give Maya useful visual information

**Observation:**
The trail cards use colored squares instead of actual photos of the trails.

**Evidence:**
Maya looks at photographs before reading the details, but the trail cards only show solid colored squares. This doesn't give her a visual idea of what the trail or scenery actually looks like.

**User Impact:**
This might make Maya less interested in the trails or have a harder time getting an idea of what to expect before choosing one. Since she looks at photos first, the current images don't really help her compare the trails.

**Principle:**
Clarity and user needs.

**Priority:** Medium

**Recommendation:**
Use meaningful visual information that helps Maya understand and compare the trails before reading the details.

### Finding 4: The trail filters don't actually work
**Observation:**
The trail cards are stacked one after another on a phone instead of showing multiple trails at once.

**Evidence:**
The desktop layout shows multiple trail cards next to each other, while the mobile layout puts them into one column.

**User Impact:**
Maya usually researches hikes on her phone, so she has to scroll up and down to compare trails. This makes it harder to remember information from one trail while looking at another.

**Principle:**
Cognitive load and mobile usability.

**Priority:** Medium

**Recommendation:**
Make the most important trail information easier to compare when using the site on a phone.  

### Finding 5: Comparing trails on mobile takes more work

**Observation:**
The trail cards are stacked one after another on a phone, which makes it harder to compare the different trails.

**Evidence:**
On a larger screen, Maya can see multiple trail cards at the same time. On a phone, the cards are shown one at a time, so she has to scroll through each trail separately. Maya also prefers information that is easy to scan and read.

**User Impact:**
Maya usually researches hikes on her phone and compares several trails before choosing one. Having to scroll back and forth makes it harder to compare things like distance, elevation, and difficulty. It also makes it harder to quickly find the information she is looking for.

**Principle:**
Mobile usability, cognitive load, and accessibility.

**Priority:** Medium

**Recommendation:**
Make the most important trail information easier to read and compare when using the site on a phone.

---

## 3. Top Three Priorities
Identify the three findings that should move forward into Week 7.

1. No estimated hiking time: This is important because Maya's main goal is finding a hike she can finish in about three hours or less. Without an estimated time she has to guess.

2. Difficulty labels don't give enough information: Since Maya is a beginner she needs to understand what the difficulty actually means. I think just seeing "Easy" or "Moderate" may not be enough for her to know if she will be comfortable on the trail.

3. Comparing trails on mobile takes more work: Maya usually researches hikes on her phone and compares  trails before choosing one. Having the trails stacked one at a time makes it harder to compare things like distance, elevation, and difficulty without scrolling back and forth.
